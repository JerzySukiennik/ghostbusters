## 0. Phase 1: what the first playable must do

Browser game, third-person, desktop keyboard + mouse. Later (phase 2) a city to drive through and online co-op for 1-4 players; phase 1 is one single-player mission on a night base plate.

1. Spawn beside the parked Ecto-1 on the plate. The car is drivable (F enter/exit, WASD), siren + roof beacon (H), real engine audio.
2. At the back of the car (E): the rear door swings open, a tray with 4 proton packs slides out, the avatar dons a pack in a cinematic animation, pulls the neutrona wand from the gun mount, the pack powers up (cyclotron lights spin, power cell climbs, whine).
3. A Slimer-class ghost ("Onionhead": green, gluttonous, mischievous) roams the plate, eats from a hot-dog cart, knocks props over, swoops at the player (slime splat on the camera).
4. Catching, TVG-style: blast with the stream to weaken it (pack heat, overheat, vent), the stream locks into a capture stream, wrangle it (fight its pull with the mouse), slam it, throw the trap, stomp the pedal, drag the ghost into the light cone, the trap snaps shut and smokes, carry the trap back to the Ecto-1. Mission complete.
5. Two looks, switched live (key V, URL `?style=modern|film`):
   - `modern`: realistic, as if made today. Bar: Ghostbusters: Afterlife (2021) proton streams and ghosts.
   - `film`: the 1984 movie's optical-composite look (hand-animated streams, puppet-like glowing ghost, optical glow, grain). Bar: Ghostbusters (1984).
   Variants change the streams, the wand/pack light behaviour, the ghost, the capture FX and an optional film grade. Ecto-1, avatar and plate are shared.

Bar for the whole loop: Ghostbusters: The Video Game Remastered (2019) for the catching feel, the Ecto-1, the packs and the suits; Ghostbusters (1984) for the film look; Ghostbusters: Afterlife (2021) for the modern look. Critics compare our frames against real frames of these, blind.

## 3. Units, frames, materials

- Metres. glTF/three.js: Y up. Characters face +Z, the character's left = +X. Vehicles and props: front = +Z.
- Blender: Z up; export glTF with `export_yup=True` (Blender -Y forward becomes glTF +Z).
- GLB: no cameras or lights inside; glTF PBR materials (KHR clearcoat / emissive_strength are fine; avoid transmission, it renders the scene twice); textures at most 2048 px (JPEG for colour/ORM without alpha, PNG for normal maps and alpha); names exactly as below.
- Emissive parts use their own materials whose names contain `Emit`.
- Segmented light groups (cyclotron, power cell, bargraph, light bars, trap lights): ONE mesh per group with a float vertex attribute `_SEG` (segment index 0..N-1; export with `export_attributes=True`; it arrives in three.js as the geometry attribute `_seg`) and put `"segments": N` into the node extras. The game lights the segments through a shader patch.
- Every moving part is its own node with its origin on the real hinge/axis. Each asset has a `.json` sidecar listing axes, angles, travel, dimensions and event times.

## 4. Asset contracts

### 4.1 Ecto-1: `assets/ecto1/ecto1.glb` + `assets/ecto1/ecto1.json`
1959 Cadillac Miller-Meteor (Futura Duplex ambulance/hearse combination) exactly as Ecto-1 in Ghostbusters (1984): clean white 1984 version (not the Afterlife rust, not Ecto-1A).
- Root node `Ecto1`. Origin on the ground (y = 0 at tyre contact), under the middle of the wheelbase, on the centreline. Front = +Z, driver side (US, left) = +X.
- Nodes (exact names): `Body` (may contain several meshes), `Wheel_FL`, `Wheel_FR`, `Wheel_RL`, `Wheel_RR` (origin at the wheel centre, spin axis = local X), `Door_FL`, `Door_FR` (origin on the hinge line), `RearDoor` (origin on its hinge), `PackTray` (+ optional `PackTray2` for a second stage), `SteeringWheel` (origin at the hub), `Beacon` (roof rotating light, spins about local Y), emissive meshes `HeadlightEmit`, `TailLightEmit`, `BeaconEmit`, `LightBarEmit` (`_SEG`), `InteriorEmit`.
- Empties: `Seat_Driver` (avatar hips pose when seated), `Exit_Driver` (avatar root pose after getting out), `PackSlot_1..4` (pack root pose on the fully extended tray, pack contact face pointing out of the back of the car, i.e. pack +Z = car -Z), `PackPickup_1..4` (avatar root pose, facing the pack, that puts `PackSlot_n` exactly at the nominal grab offset of section 4.3), `TrapStow` (where the full trap goes in the back), `Siren`, `Exhaust`, `Headlight_L`, `Headlight_R` (spot origins, local +Z forward).
- Physics: hidden convex collider meshes named `Collider*` (each at most 64 vertices).
- `ecto1.json`: `wheelbase`, `track_front`, `track_rear`, `wheel_radius`, `wheel_width`, `mass_kg`, `ride_height`, door/rear door hinge axis + open angle (sign!), tray axis + travel (+ second stage), steering wheel axis + ratio, beacon spin axis, all empties listed with their world pose.
- Livery and roof rack per the 1984 film; No-Ghost logos where the film car has them; `ECTO-1` plates front and rear.
- Budget at most 150k triangles, at most 30 materials, GLB at most 20 MB. Interior visible through the glass (seats, dash, steering wheel, rear compartment, tray).

### 4.2 Pack, wand, trap: `assets/pack/protonpack.glb`, `wand.glb`, `trap.glb` + `assets/pack/pack.json`
The 1984 film hero proton pack (ALICE frame) and neutrona wand; the 1984 ghost trap with its foot pedal on a cable.
- `protonpack.glb`, root `ProtonPack`. Origin = centre of the frame's contact face (the plane touching the wearer's back), at the frame's vertical centre. Contact face normal = +Z (toward the wearer), the pack extends toward -Z. Up = +Y. The wearer's right = -X. Rough bounds: x within +-0.23, y within +-0.36, z from 0 to -0.30.
  Empties: `GunMount` (the wand root pose when docked), `HoseAnchor` (hose start, empty +Z = direction the hose leaves), `Vent` (N-filter, +Y = steam direction), `StrapTop_L`, `StrapTop_R`, `StrapBottom_L`, `StrapBottom_R`, `Belt_L`, `Belt_R` (where the harness attaches), `Handle` (top grab point).
  Emissive groups: `CyclotronEmit` (4 segments, red, 1984 style), `PowerCellEmit` (15 segments, blue, bottom to top), `PackLightsEmit` (misc small lights).
  `HoseSample`: hidden 0.2 m straight ribbed hose along +Z with the real hose material; `pack.json` gives the hose radius. The game builds the actual hose between the two `HoseAnchor`s.
- `wand.glb`, root `Wand`. Origin = palm centre of the right hand on the rear (trigger) grip. Barrel forward = +Z, up = +Y. Empties: `GripFront` (left palm centre and orientation), `Tip` (emitter, +Z forward), `HoseAnchor`. Emissive: `BargraphEmit` (`_SEG`, segment count in extras), `WandLightsEmit` (`_SEG`: 0 slo-blo, 1 top, 2 vent, 3 side; document in json), `TipEmit` (flasher at the barrel tip).
- `trap.glb`, root `Trap`. Origin = bottom centre, front = +Z. Nodes `DoorL`, `DoorR` (hinge origins, open angle in json), `Pedal` (separate root-level node, the game places it on the ground), `CableAnchor` (on the trap), `PedalAnchor` (on the pedal), `BeamOrigin` (empty, +Y up), `Smoke_1..n` (empties), emissive `TrapEmit` (`_SEG`, documented).
- `pack.json`: dimensions, all empty poses, hose radius, segment maps, door angles, masses (pack ~14 kg, trap ~4 kg).
- Budgets: pack at most 40k tris, wand at most 15k, trap at most 10k. Real wear in the materials.

### 4.4 VFX: `src/fx/` + `fx-lab.html`
API (exact):
```js
import { FX } from './src/fx/index.js';
const fx = new FX({ renderer, scene, camera, quality, style });  // creates its lights here, never later
await fx.ready;                          // shaders for both styles compiled
fx.setStyle('modern' | 'film');          // live, no hitch
const s = fx.createStream();             // load time only
s.update(dt, { on, mode, origin, dir, end, endNormal, endKind, heat, tension, ghostPos });
//   mode 'blast' | 'capture'; endKind 'ghost' | 'surface' | 'air'; heat, tension 0..1
const g = fx.createGhost();              // Onionhead, load time only
g.object3D; g.radius;
g.update(dt, { vel, mood, hurt, struggle, suck, trapPos, mouth });
//   mood 'idle' | 'eat' | 'taunt' | 'angry' | 'flee' | 'hurt' | 'wrangled' | 'sucked'
const t = fx.createTrapFX(trapObject3D); // load time only
t.update(dt, { open, beam, suck, closed, smoking });   // 0..1 / booleans
fx.impact(position, normal, kind);       // 'spark' | 'scorch' | 'slime' | 'ghostHit' | 'slam' | 'debris'
fx.slimeScreen(amount);                  // slime splat on the camera, 0..1
fx.update(dt, time);
fx.postPasses();                         // [{ name, pass }] extra composer passes (film grade, heat haze); may be empty
```
All vectors world space. The ghost's look, deformation, particles, decals and the trap beam are FX's; the ghost's movement and AI are the lead's. Budgets at 1920x1080 on the MBP 2019 (Medium): stream at most 1.5 ms GPU, ghost at most 1.5 ms, trap FX at most 0.7 ms, at most 4 PointLights owned by FX in total, zero allocations per frame, zero shader compiles after `ready`.

## 5. Physics groups (Rapier interaction groups)
```
G_STATIC  = 0x0001   G_PROP  = 0x0002   G_PLAYER = 0x0004   G_VEHICLE = 0x0008
G_DEBRIS  = 0x0010   G_GHOST = 0x0020   G_TRAP   = 0x0040   G_SENSOR  = 0x0080
interactionGroups(member, filter) = (member << 16) | filter
```
Static world: member G_STATIC, filter 0xFFFF. Props: member G_PROP, filter 0xFFFF. Debris: member G_DEBRIS, filter G_STATIC | G_PROP | G_VEHICLE. The lead's `src/engine/physics.js` exports the same constants.
